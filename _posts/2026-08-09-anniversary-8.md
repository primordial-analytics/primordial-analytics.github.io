## Happy 8th Anniversary

*And Maynard read unto them: on the eighth count shalt thou raise the Holy Spreadsheet of Moderate Confidence, and lo, it shall have no missing values; nine is right out.*

Today, we celebrate Primordial Analytics' eighth anniversary, and we are grateful to be here to do it! This one is quieter than most, because it has been a hard year for the community health organizations we exist to serve, and we want to talk about that before we talk about cake.

### A Quiet Inbox

Fewer people asked for our volunteer data science help this year. That makes sense. When you are cutting staff and consolidating sites, even free analytics costs something you don't have: the hours of a person to explain the data and act on it. A free dashboard still needs a human to say yes.

If your answer this year was "not right now," that was the right answer. We would much rather be quiet than add one more item to an overloaded to-do list. Our inbox is one small data point, but the larger numbers tell a similar story.

### What Is Coming for Coverage

Some of the changes have already landed. The enhanced ACA premium tax credits expired at the end of 2025. The House passed a three-year extension in January; the Senate, which had already fallen short of 60 votes on a similar bill in December, had not taken it up as of this writing. [KFF reports](https://www.kff.org/quick-insights/aca-marketplace-enrollment-is-down-by-3-million-after-big-jump-in-premium-payments/) that net premium payments rose 58% on average for people who signed up for 2026, and paid Marketplace enrollment fell.

The biggest Medicaid changes are still ahead. The CBO projected that the 2025 reconciliation law (H.R. 1) alone would leave [about 10 million more people uninsured by 2034](https://ccf.georgetown.edu/2025/08/14/new-cbo-health-coverage-estimates-of-budget-reconciliation-law/). CMS issued its interim work-requirement rule in early June, with a January 1, 2027 deadline for states (a few started earlier), and a federal judge declined to pause it in late July, though the case continues. Six-month renewals, provider-tax changes, and cost sharing phase in after that. As forecasts go, it is a cliff with unusually good date labels and, as always, wide error bars on what happens at the bottom.

For a health center, coverage losses show up as patients who keep coming and can no longer pay, and as new paperwork: tracking work requirements and redoing renewals every six months.

### Money Is Tight Everywhere

Health centers were already averaging a [-2% margin in 2024](https://www.nachc.org/the-coming-surge-in-uninsured-patients-at-community-health-centers/), according to NACHC, before most of this began. The Community Health Center Fund, their largest source of federal grant money, is funded only through December 31, 2026. A House committee voted unanimously in July to advance new behavioral health and nutrition funding for health centers. It has not become law, and it isn't a substitute for the Fund.

The wider nonprofit world feels it too. In a [Center for Effective Philanthropy survey](https://cep.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/CEP_State_of_Nonprofits_2026_FNL.pdf) of nonprofit CEOs fielded in February, 73% reported higher demand since January 2025, while many had cut staff or services. More work, fewer hands. In Oregon, Lane County [announced it would cut 23 positions](https://lookouteugene-springfield.com/story/health/2026/06/24/lane-county-says-it-will-cut-23-positions-at-its-safety-net-primary-care-clinics/) at its safety-net clinics in June, eight of them vacant, citing the expired ACA credits and federal Medicaid changes. In Indiana, two health centers announced a merger in July, and one CEO pointed to Medicaid cuts.

Through all of it, the people at these organizations keep showing up for their patients, and we are in awe of that. Showing up shouldn't have to be this heavy a lift, and we don't mistake your resilience for a plan.

### Looking Ahead: Year Nine

We don't have a grand expansion plan this year, and we think that's the right call. Tell us what would actually help, even if the answer is "nothing right now," and we'll shape year nine around that. In the meantime, we hope to be useful in smaller, lighter ways:

- **[Small asks]({{ site.baseurl }}/community-health-data-help/)**: A single chart, a spreadsheet sanity check, or a short call can beat a big project that no one has time to host.
- **Medicaid preparation**: If it would help, we can sanity-check whatever you've already built, or run a simple what-if on your own patient data before the dates arrive.
- **Open tools**: Sharing what we build so a busy team can pick it up on a Tuesday afternoon without a kickoff meeting.

Most of all, we plan to stay available. If you run a community health organization and a question has been nagging at you, send it our way. Our help is free, and you don't owe us a reply.

To our volunteers, partners, and the people who keep the lights on: thank you for eight years. We don't know exactly what year nine holds. We'll be here, and glad to be.
