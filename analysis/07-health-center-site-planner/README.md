# Health Center Site Planner

An example on public data for health center planning teams: which ZIP codes hold the most low-income residents who are not health center patients, how sure we are, and, for a new site at a given ZIP with a given capacity, who its patients would be (people with no center today or taken from neighbours) and with what payer mix. No patient information. Capacity is an input: the model does not forecast how many patients a site draws.

Run: `pip install -r requirements.txt && python run.py` (about 2.5 hours on a 16-core laptop the first time (the 100 bootstrap refits take most of it), 5 minutes with the cache in `data/raw/cache/`; delete the `fits_*.pkl` files there to refit; set `PYTHONDONTWRITEBYTECODE=1` so no `__pycache__` lands in the repo). LibreOffice (`soffice`) is optional: with it the workbook carries cached results.

- `fetch.py` downloads the raw files to `data/raw/` (gitignored) and keeps `data/raw/SOURCES.txt` (URL, bytes, sha256, first download date).
- `prepare.py` cleans them: ZIPs as text, '--' censored cells, the ZIP-to-ZCTA crosswalk, ACS tables, site list, the exact hidden sum per center.
- `hidden.py` places the censored cells given each center's exact hidden total (exact conditional sampler).
- `model.py` is the competitive gravity model (JAX, maximum likelihood); `fits.py` runs the fits in parallel (main, 100 bootstrap refits, checks).
- `reach.py` is the accounting: patients per ZIP, low-income residents who are not patients, Monte Carlo on ACS margins, payer mix.
- `project.py` is the new-site projection. `results.py` builds the ZIP table and the headline aggregates.
- `validate.py` runs checks A to F. `workbook.py` builds the Excel screen. `web_data.py` writes the page data. `sources.py` writes `output/facts.json`.
- Every number on the case-study page is in `output/numbers.json`; the policy facts are in `output/facts.json`.

Sources (downloaded 2026-10-23): HRSA UDS 2025 and 2024 awardee workbooks (patients by ZIP and payer, Table 4); HRSA health center site list; HRSA Unmet Need Score file (ZIP-to-ZCTA crosswalk and HRSA's published penetration, a comparator only); Census ACS 2020-2024 5-year tables C17002 and C27016; Census 2024 Gazetteer; Census 2020 ZCTA-to-county file; HRSA bulletin of 2026-08-27 and the CMS community engagement rule (Federal Register 2026-11094) for the two background facts.

## Method in one paragraph

Expected patients from ZCTA z to center c: `lam = L_z * theta_c * f_k(d) / (1 + S_z)`, with `S_z` the sum of `theta * f` over all centers (the 1 is the outside option, people who use no center), `L_z` low-income residents, `d` straight-line miles to the center's nearest permanent site, `f_k(d) = exp(-(d / rho_k)^gamma_k)` with one `(rho, gamma)` per density class of the ZCTA, and `log theta_c = mu_type + beta * log(sites) + sigma_type * eps_c` (a random effect per center). Poisson likelihood on visible cells, zeros within 60 miles included, and P(1 <= Y <= 15) on censored cells. Uncertainty: 100 refits of the model with every center's rows weighted by an independent Exponential(1) draw (a Bayesian cluster bootstrap over centers: no center is dropped), with the ACS margins drawn in. Look-alike sites are competitors at a typical theta; their patients are not in the UDS awardee file.

New site at x, capacity K, `theta` of a typical single site (the type level at one site plus the median center effect of the fitted single-site centers of that type, because single-site centers sit below the size trend; `eps = 0` would overstate it by about a third), `w_z = theta f(d_xz)`: draw from z `L_z w_z / (1 + S_z + w_z)`; newly reached (taken from the outside option) `L_z w_z / ((1 + S_z)(1 + S_z + w_z))`; the rest comes from neighbours, and neighbour c loses `lam_cz w_z / (1 + S_z + w_z)`. If the draw D exceeds K every flow is scaled by K / D. The model's draw is not credible as a volume (a typical single site would draw more than 5,000 patients in most ZIPs, against a median real site of about 2,700), so no uncapped draw is published: capacity is an input and the outputs that do not depend on it (share new, payer mix, sources, neighbour losses per patient) are the result. Payer mix: neighbours' patients keep their ZIP's observed mix (Dirichlet-multinomial shrinkage toward the state and density class), new patients are uninsured at the ACS uninsured share of low-income residents of all ages in the ZIP and the insured remainder follows the ZIP's observed pattern.

## Page data format (`assets/examples/07/`)

`data.json`: the 24 parameter sets (`sets`: rho and gamma by density class, theta of a typical single site by center type), `centers` (service-area totals within 40 miles), `lookalikes` (names of the entities after the grantees, so neighbour indices of 1338 and above resolve), `type_labels`, `columns` (the manifest of `zcta.bin.gz`), `states`, `counties`, `facts`. `zcta.bin.gz`: gzip of little-endian columns laid end to end, each `{name, dtype, offset, length}` in bytes. Decoding used by `web_data.decode`: `lat = lat/1e4`, `lon = lon/1e4`, payer mixes `/1e4`, `S0 = expm1(S0/5500)`, set b: `S_b = (S0 + 0.01) * exp(S_b/400) - 0.01`, `L_b = max((low + 10) * exp(L_b/24) - 10, 0)`, neighbour `lam = (nbr_lam/200)^2`, 65535 means missing. `centers_top_zips.json`: each center's 20 ZIPs with most patients (indices into the ZIP columns).
