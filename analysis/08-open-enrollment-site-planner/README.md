# Open Enrollment Site Planner

An example on public data, not client work: uninsured people in every census tract, split by what they could enroll in, with 90% ranges, and a planner that picks 1 to 8 enrollment event sites to reach the most of them. No personal information. Model outputs are estimate ranges, not forecasts.

Run: `pip install -r requirements.txt && OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 python -I run.py` (about 2 to 5 minutes once the files are downloaded, longer on a busy machine; the first run downloads about 900 MB, mostly the 2024 person-level survey file). `workbook.py` needs LibreOffice (`soffice`) to store calculated values in the example workbook; without it the workbook is saved with formulas only and says so. `--cache` also saves the page-data inputs, so `python -I web_data.py` rebuilds `assets/examples/08/` alone. `--cache` keeps the slow intermediate results in `data/raw/_cache` for quick reruns while editing.

- `fetch.py` downloads raw files to `data/raw/` (gitignored) and keeps `data/raw/SOURCES.txt` (URL, bytes, sha256, first download date). Downloads are treated as untrusted: zip paths are checked, nothing is executed, and all Python runs with `-I`.
- `sources.py` holds the policy facts (open enrollment dates, the states with their own marketplace, navigator funding, the Medicaid expansion list) with their URLs and the date checked, and writes `output/facts.json`.
- `prepare.py` builds the 84,401-tract table (50 states and DC; Puerto Rico is dropped), the candidate venues and the CMS plan selections.
- `pums.py` calibrates the noncitizen share of the uninsured by state and income band from the ACS 2024 1-year person file.
- `sae.py` is the small-area model; `tiers.py` turns its draws into tiers; `estimate.py` runs it county by county.
- `sites.py` and `plans.py` are the planner's rule (the browser runs the same greedy rule) and the strategy comparison.
- `validate.py` and `simulate.py` hold the checks. `results.py` assembles `output/numbers.json`, the source of every number on the page.
- `tables.py`, `workbook.py` and `web_data.py` write the downloads in `downloads/08-open-enrollment-site-planner-*` and the page data in `assets/examples/08/`.

## Method

1. **Tract table.** ACS 2020-2024 table C27016 gives, for each tract, insured and uninsured counts for children and for adults 19 to 64 in five bands of income to poverty (under 100%, 100-137%, 138-199%, 200-399%, 400% and over). Margins of error become standard errors (MOE / 1.645) and are added in quadrature, counting only the largest margin among zero estimates once. B27020 gives noncitizen uninsured (all ages). CDC PLACES 2025 (lack of insurance, adults 18-64) is a covariate with a missing flag (Kentucky and Pennsylvania are absent). SAHIE 2024 county rates are a covariate, matched to the band. A one-year age mismatch (SAHIE adults are 18-64) is stated. The `population` column is the civilian noninstitutionalized population with poverty status determined, a little below the resident population.
2. **Model.** Four targets: children, adults at or below 138%, adults 138-399%, adults 400% and over. For each target and each Census region, an area-level Fay-Herriot model on the rate scale: the direct rate is the true rate plus sampling error, and the true rate is a linear function of the covariates plus a county effect plus a tract effect. Covariates: PLACES rate and missing flag, SAHIE county rate, noncitizen share of the uninsured, share of residents under 138% of poverty, expansion state, density class, the cell's size (log n and its square), the cell's share of the tract's people, and the pooled rate of the six nearest tracts. The sampling variance of a cell is a scaled Poisson with a binomial correction, `var(count) = W x count`, so `var(rate) = W p / n`: W (the survey's weight per unit) is a generalized variance function of cell size and rate, fit as `log W = a + b log n + c log(1 - p)` on the cells with a usable published SE (`W = SE^2 / count`), evaluated at the county's pooled rate so that it does not move with the tract's own estimate. It is used for every cell, floored at its 25th percentile and capped at three times its mean where a published SE is used; a zero count or a zero margin takes the same function, because the published margin on a zero is a survey-wide constant. Variances are fit by REML. The tract-level variance is weakly identified (sampling noise is about ten times larger), so the model uses the upper end of its one-sided 95% profile-likelihood interval, found by root finding between the REML value and the total variance (`numbers.json` keeps both under `model_fit`); it is then floored at the moment estimate from the best-measured quarter of tracts. 1,000 draws per tract come from the conditional normal distributions, with coefficients drawn from their estimated distribution. The coefficient draws and the county effect are shared by the four targets, so the sum of children and two adult bands is not treated as a sum of unrelated things; tract effects are independent between targets.
3. **Calibration.** The model's county totals fall below the sum of the tracts' own counts (reported in `numbers.json` under `calibration`, with the survey's and the model's mean rate by quintile of sampling variance). The cause is that precision-weighted fitting follows the well-measured cells, and the cells the survey measures worst have much higher direct rates, so the model's mean rate is lower than the survey's. Adding log n squared to the covariates (a curve in cell size) did not move the scale factors noticeably, so the pattern is not a simple function of cell size that the model could learn; it is left to the county scale factors. Each county and target is scaled by `(U + 100) / (M + 100 / f_region)`: U the direct total, M the model total, f_region the region's own ratio. The sums are repaired; the shape inside a county comes from the model.
4. **SAHIE soft benchmark.** If a county's mean for adults at or below 138%, adults 138-399% or children is outside SAHIE's 90% interval it moves to the nearest edge; then adults 400% and over adjust so the all-income adult total is inside SAHIE's interval. The benchmark moves a majority of counties, and `numbers.json` reports the survey's tract sums against SAHIE (`acs_vs_sahie`): the two sources disagree by more than the model's draw interval.
5. **Tiers.** Adults at or below 138% are split at 100% of poverty from the tract's own two bands, shrunk (30 people) to a prior built from the state's ratio of the bands' uninsured rates; the split has no interval of its own. Expansion states: Medicaid range (at or below 138%), subsidy range (138-399%). Other states: coverage gap (under 100%), subsidy range (100-399%). Wisconsin's adults under 100% are placed in the Medicaid range and flagged (BadgerCare covers them under a waiver). Georgia's Pathways program has a work requirement and very small enrollment, and KFF lists Georgia as a state with a coverage gap, so Georgia is treated like the other gap states and Pathways is a text note. Children under 400% of poverty are one tier. A tract's share of uninsured children at 400% and over (its own cell, shrunk to its county with a pseudo-count of 10 children) goes to the 400% tier with the adults: those children are mostly above the Medicaid and CHIP limits and get no premium credit. Noncitizen adults are removed from every adult tier into their own tier, 'eligibility varies': the tract's noncitizen share of the uninsured (all ages, shrunk to its county) is moved by the state-and-band odds ratio from the 2024 person file (each state's log odds ratio is shrunk toward its Census division's with weight n / (n + 30), n the sampled noncitizens in the band), with a plus or minus 30% odds sensitivity. Tiers are probabilities from a model, not statements about a person. They use income only: work rules, employer offers, household income as the programs define it and immigration status beyond the carve-out are not tested.
6. **The headline range.** The draw interval is sampling and model noise given the model's choices. The range of the claim is the minimum and maximum of the SAHIE benchmark on or off, times the noncitizen odds at 1/1.3, 1 and 1.3 (`national.scenarios`).
7. **Tract ranges.** In the simulation (validation D) each tier's tract 90% range is checked against the true values. A tier whose range covered less than 88% is widened around the tract mean by the factor that gives 90% (`validation.D_simulation.tract_interval_scale`); the coverage after widening is published next to every `_lo90`/`_hi90` column in the codebook. County and national intervals are not widened.
8. **Sites.** Expected reach of a plan is the sum over tracts of expected eligible people times `min(1, sum of site weights)`, where a site's weight for a tract is `exp(-2 (d / R)^2)` up to R miles (3 urban, 5 suburban, 15 rural; d is straight-line miles x 1.3). A tract at the limit counts 14% of its people, so this is a distance-weighted count, not a headcount of people who will come. The cap at 1 is the competing-site discount. The objective is submodular, so greedy is near-optimal; the browser runs greedy and `plans.py` solves each of 50 counties exactly as a mixed-integer program (HiGHS) to report the gap. The range for a plan is cut at 0 and at the county total. In the browser, groups other than the default use the sum of the groups' spreads, so their range is an upper bound. The interval for a plan combines tract ranges as if independent and multiplies by a county factor (the square root of the mean ratio of the true variance of the reach to the independent variance, over the greedy plans of 3, 5 and 8 sites). It is for the default settings. Candidate venues: IMLS FY2023 library outlets (no bookmobiles; some will have closed) and HRSA permanent health center sites that deliver services (not administrative-only offices), are open 20 or more hours a week and are not hospital, school, nursing home, shelter or correctional settings. A venue belongs to the county its own file gives (IMLS tract code, HRSA county code), not the nearest tract's. Tract centres as pop-ups are a separate option.

## Validation (results in `output/numbers.json` under `validation`)

- **A. Masked-tract holdout.** 20% of tracts with 3,000 or more people are withheld at random and the model is refit without them (a second run withholds tracts of any size). The weight function and each county's pooled rate use only the tracts that were not withheld, and so does the county calibration. The 90% predictive interval for each withheld tract (model plus a simulated survey report, whose error is a scaled Poisson so small cells can read zero) is checked against the tract's own reported count, which is itself noisy: coverage overall, by region and density, and for tracts whose own estimate is good (CV below 20%; few tracts for children and for the low-income band). Compared with PLACES alone and with the county rate on RMSE and top-decile precision.
- **B. Independent county benchmark.** The model is refit without SAHIE as a covariate and without the benchmark, summed to counties, and compared with SAHIE 2024. The refit is still calibrated to ACS county totals, so this mostly tests the ACS against SAHIE. Reported as rates (median absolute gap in percentage points) and interval overlap; the log-count correlation is driven by county size. The production model's agreement is reported too and labelled not independent.
- **C. Replication on the 2015-2019 survey** was not run (the tract crosswalk was not attempted).
- **D. Simulation inside the model.** Truth is drawn from the fitted model, a survey report is simulated for every tract (including zero cells), and the whole pipeline is rerun: bias, coverage of every tier by tract, rank correlation, and how many tracts can be told apart from their county's median, for the model and for the survey's own counts, also at equal 90% coverage. These are results for a world the model describes.
- **E. Site plans.** (1) Plans from the model's estimates versus plans from the survey's own counts, scored on simulated truth in 20 counties (regret table in `numbers.json`); (2) the planner's interval against a Monte Carlo interval for greedy 5- and 8-site plans, a 5-site plan at the largest distance and random plans in the same 20 counties; (3) greedy against the exact optimum; (4) ways to choose sites in the 300 counties with the most tracts among those with 8 or more venues. In (4) every strategy is scored with the optimiser's own reach rule on the model's expected people, so the optimiser wins by construction; (1) is the independent check. A spacing test (the count order with sites at least one travel distance apart) and a no-overlap-discount score are reported.
- **F. Scenarios** (not validated, labelled): the share of noncitizen adults able to get help (default 0), the plan-selection slider, the 1.3 circuity factor. The person-file check on the noncitizen share uses the same ratios, so it is a consistency check.

## Limits

An example on public data, not client work. Survey years are 2020-2024 and predate the 2026 drop in enrollment. Noncitizens are shown apart because the data cannot tell who is eligible. Distance is straight-line, with no transit, hours or language. Libraries and health center sites stand in for where assisters can meet people; existing assister sites are not known (CMS Find Local Help has no bulk file). Tract ranks are guidance: many tracts cannot be told apart. PLACES is itself modelled from ACS covariates. The Medicaid expansion list, the enrollment dates and the 2027 federal rules (work requirements, immigrant eligibility; not modelled) are typed constants with sources and dates. Tract ranges are guidance. The SAHIE comparison, the choice of Georgia and Wisconsin and the noncitizen odds are the largest sources of uncertainty in the totals, and they are why the page gives a range, not the interval of the draws.
